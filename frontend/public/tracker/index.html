Verification: how these forecasts did in past seasons

RPSS compares the early / normal / late probabilities with always forecasting one-third each (above 0 = better than that baseline). Hit rate = share of past seasons in which the most likely category happened (baseline 0.33). Scores come from hindcasts at the station's grid cell for the same run and method; a small sample, so treat them as rough.

Method · variableVerification periodRPSSHit rateBaseline (climatology)Sample

Methods. Dunning: turning points of the accumulated rainfall anomaly, 1 Sep – 31 Dec. Agri rule 1: onset on a wet day (≥1 mm) starting more than 20 mm in 3 days, with no dry spell of 7 days or more in the next 21 days. Rule 2: at least 20 mm in 5 days with 3 or more rainy days, no dry spell longer than 7 days in the next 21. Rule 3: more than 20 mm in 3 days, no dry spell longer than 10 days in the next 30. Cessation (rules 1–2): after a 10-day period with rain below half the potential evapotranspiration (modified Hargreaves), the day a 100 mm soil store runs dry; rule 3: the first day of that 10-day period. FAO water balance (where available; CHIRPS v3 rainfall, FAO-56 Penman-Monteith ET0 from ERA5-Land, maize water need, SoilGrids soil water): agricultural onset when 10-day rain reaches half the atmospheric demand (ET0) and plant-available water stays at ≥20% of what the root zone holds for 5 days with no 7-day fall below it in the next 21; agricultural cessation after 7 days of critically low soil water (<20%), crop water use below half its need and a 10-day rain deficit. Rainfall onset and cessation use rain against ET0 only; crop-water cessation is when 21 days of rain fall short of half the crop's water need after the seasonal peak. Each definition has its own season length. Agri rules 1–3 follow ICPAC's operational onset/cessation definitions. They search onset from 15 Aug to 15 Jan and cessation to 28 Feb (the 1 Oct runs carry the forecast to 28 Feb; the 1 Sep run stops at 31 Dec); members with none by then count as late.

5. How these numbers are made

Seasonal forecast. ECMWF SEAS5 started 1 October 2026, 25 members, bias-corrected day by day against CHIRPS 1993–2016 by quantile mapping. Observed CHIRPS rainfall replaces the forecast up to the latest observed day in every year, so skill is measured the same way it is used.

Statistics shown. The median estimate is the middle value of the ensemble members (members with no event by the end of the search count as later than any date). The middle 50% of members runs from the 25th to the 75th percentile, the middle 80% from the 10th to the 90th.

Onset and cessation methods. Dunning onset and cessation are the turning points of accumulated rainfall anomaly over 1 Sep – 31 Dec. The seasonal outlook also gives three agricultural rules, Agri rules 1–3, adapted from ICPAC's operational definitions (onset from 15 Aug; cessation from 10-day rain against half the modified-Hargreaves potential evapotranspiration, with a 100 mm soil store for rules 1 and 2), described under Season outlook. In the weather forecasts, planting rains are at least 20 mm within 3 days.

Probabilities and skill. Early, normal and late are the thirds of the 1993–2016 CHIRPS record at each cell, calibrated toward one-third each where past forecasts were weak. Skill compares past forecasts at the site's cell with CHIRPS (RPSS and hit rate; see “Show verification details” under Season outlook).

Satellite products. CHIRPS (preliminary after September), ARC2, TAMSAT v3.1, PDIR-Now and NASA IMERG Late and Early, averaged onto the same 0.25° grid. GSMaP is not included yet (needs an Earth Engine project or a JAXA account). Each product is described under Data sources below.

Seasonal conditioning. Where no product shows an onset, members with an onset before the ensemble start date are dropped and the probabilities recomputed.
